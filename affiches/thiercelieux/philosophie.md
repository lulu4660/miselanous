# Nocturne Clinique

**Philosophy:** the night observed like a specimen. Fear is not illustrated, it is *recorded*: measured, dated, plotted. Ink and bone on black; one glowing body of blood-orange light; everything else is the instrument that watches it.

**Space and form.** A single monumental disc dominates the field, cropped by nothing, held in place by a strict margin system that never wavers. Around it, the architecture of observation: hairlines, ticks, a horizontal sequence of small identical circles that progress patiently toward the large one. Geometry is perfect or it is absent: circles are true circles, rules are true rules, and the only irregular forms are the ones nature makes, a jagged horizon of roofs and trees, and three cuts that tear through the light. The composition must read as meticulously crafted, every distance chosen, every edge resolved with painstaking attention.

**Colour and material.** Three inks only: a soot black that is never pure, a bone white that has aged, and a single blood orange reserved for what matters. Surfaces carry the memory of print: a fine halftone in the disc, a breath of grain across the dark, as if pulled from a press by someone who has spent a lifetime calibrating ink. No gradient is decorative; each one describes light falling on a surface.

**Scale and rhythm.** Extreme contrast of size. One word is enormous; the labels around it are tiny, set in a clinical monospace, the voice of a field notebook. Repetition builds tension: the same mark again and again until the eye anticipates the next. The rhythm should feel like a held breath, the master-level execution of a few elements rather than the accumulation of many.

**Composition and hierarchy.** Read the poster from across a room and it gives you three things, in order: the light, the name, the time. Come close and it rewards you with the apparatus: indices, numbers, quiet annotations that make the uncanny feel documented. Nothing overlaps that should not touch; nothing falls off the page. The final artefact should look like the product of deep expertise and countless hours of refinement: a page from an imaginary science of the night.
